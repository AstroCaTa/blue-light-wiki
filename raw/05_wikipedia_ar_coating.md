# Anti-reflective coating (Optical Coating Principles)

- Source: Wikipedia — Anti-reflective coating
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-reflective_coating

## What Is an Anti-Reflective (AR) Coating?
- An anti-reflective (AR) or anti-glare coating is a thin optical film applied to lenses and optical elements to **reduce surface reflection** and increase light transmission.
- For uncoated common crown glass ($n_S \approx 1.5$) in air ($n_0 \approx 1.0$), Fresnel equations give a single-surface reflectance $R \approx 4\%$ (combined front + back window loss $\approx 7.7\%$).
- High-index eyeglass lenses reflect even more light when uncoated, making AR coatings especially beneficial for corrective eyewear.

## Physical Principle: Thin-Film Interference
- **Index matching (Rayleigh's film):** An intermediate film with refractive index $n_1 = \sqrt{n_0 n_S}$ (ideally $n_1 \approx 1.225$ for $n_S = 1.5$) splits one air-glass reflection into two weaker reflections, halving reflection loss.
- **Quarter-wave interference coating:** Coating thickness is controlled to one-quarter of the design wavelength inside the film ($\lambda/4 = \lambda_0 / (4n_1)$). Light reflected from the second interface travels half a wavelength farther than light reflected from the first interface, causing **destructive interference** that cancels reflection.
- **Single-layer material:** Magnesium fluoride ($\text{MgF}_2$, $n = 1.38$) is widely used because it is durable and reduces crown glass reflectance from ~4% to ~1%.
- **Multi-layer broadband coatings:** Alternating layers of low-index (e.g., silica) and high-index materials achieve reflectance below **0.5%** across the visible spectrum (400–700 nm), or below 0.1% at a target wavelength.

## Why Coated Eyeglass Lenses Have a Tinted Reflection
- Because thin-film interference depends on wavelength and incident angle, real coatings perform slightly differently across colors.
- Common AR coatings on eyeglasses often show a faint **bluish, greenish, or pinkish** reflection—indicating the wavelengths slightly more reflected than others.
- Note: Corrective AR lenses should not be confused with **polarized lenses** (used in sunglasses to absorb glare reflected off water, snow, or roads).

# Are Blue Light-Blocking Glasses Worth It?

- Source: American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO)
- URL: https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/are-computer-glasses-worth-it
- Published: 2021-03-05 (By Celia Vimont, Reviewed by Rahul N. Khurana, MD)

## Official Position
- There is **no scientific evidence** that the light coming from computer screens is damaging to the eyes.
- Therefore, the American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) **does not recommend** blue light-blocking glasses or any special eyewear for computer use.
- Several studies suggest that blue light-blocking glasses do not improve symptoms of digital eye strain.

## What Actually Causes Screen Eye Strain?
- Staring at digital screens for long hours leads to **decreased blinking**, which causes temporary symptoms known as digital eye strain.
- These symptoms are caused by **how people use screens**, not by the light emitted from the screens.
- A study reprinted by the National Library of Medicine found **no measurable UVA or UVB radiation** from computer screens.

## Sleep & Circadian Rhythm
- There is some evidence that blue light affects the body's **circadian rhythm** (wake and sleep cycle).
- The best way to avoid sleep disruption is to avoid screens **2 to 3 hours before bed**, or use "dark" or "night" mode on devices in the evening.

## Recommended Ways to Ease Eye Strain
- Sit about **25 inches** (an arm's length) from the screen, positioned so you gaze slightly downward.
- Follow the **20-20-20 rule**: every **20 minutes**, look at an object at least **20 feet** away for at least **20 seconds**.
- Use artificial tears when eyes feel dry, adjust room lighting, increase screen contrast, or use a matte screen filter if needed.
- Give eyes a break from contact lenses by wearing regular glasses.
